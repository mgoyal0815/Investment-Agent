System A: US v5 rebuilt with India v8's rules and a profit lock

From January 2000, $10,000 grows to about $2.27 million: 22.5% a year against 8 to 12% for SPY, QQQ and SMH, with a worst fall of −39% against their −55% to −84%. It beats all three indexes in 15 of 26 calendar years, not every year.

Where the money comes from: a few long-held winners make all the profit. System A keeps them longer (v5 adds to winners), locks gains once a position is up 30% (sell 25% below its peak), and keeps the India buy pause that protects it in crashes.

The years it still loses are SMH boom years and rebounds after a fall (2009, 2019, 2023). Every fix tried for those years, including pausing less and a rebound mode, either deepened the falls or lowered growth, so they stay as the known weakness.

What changed the US system, and what did not

What System A changes in v5

500 most-traded stocks; India's buy pause instead of the regime overlay (HYG, large-cap trend, breadth under 30%); decisions twice a month; also sell outside the top 45; idle cash in the leading index ETF, Treasuries while paused; v5 adds to winners; profit lock at +30% with a 25% trail.

How it was found

Trade-by-trade attribution on 2012-19: positions held over 63 days made all the profit, winners gave back 28 to 54 points before their stop. The two fixes for that, chosen on 2012-19 only, raised 2020-26 growth from 32.7% to 46.2% a year.

What it does not do

Beat SPY, QQQ and SMH every year. It misses SMH boom years (2003, 2009, 2019, 2023) and 2010-11. Its worst fall is smaller than SMH's from every start date tested, but not always smaller than SPY's.
