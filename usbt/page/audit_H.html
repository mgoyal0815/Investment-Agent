Audit · System H · US backtest 2000 – 12 Oct 2026

System H audit

Eleven checks for look-ahead, survivorship, execution, data errors and overfitting. The engine is clean. The data has a survivorship gap in the early years, and H's drawdown result is fragile.

Verdict

H's trading rules use only information available on the day, and the trades are executed and costed realistically. Its edge over System G is real: random restart dates do worse every time. But two things make the headline numbers look better than they should:

  • Up to a third of S&P 500 members in the early 2000s have no price data, so the 2000–11 results are flattered by an unknown amount.
  • H's settings sit on a narrow peak. Its drawdown record collapses with small changes to its settings, or when four extra stocks are added to the market breadth count.

On the corrected data, H beats SPY and QQQ in 59.0% of years from every monthly start (G: 54.8%). But its worst fall is smaller than both indexes' on only 43% of start dates (G: 78%).

Findings

Major

Survivorship gap in the early years

A stock with no price data can never be bought in the backtest. The share of S&P 500 members with no data by year:

20002004200820112014201620192022+
37.5%28.8%19.8%13.4%9.9%5.7%1.6%≤0.5%

Most of the 302 missing names were bought out (Heinz, Wyeth, EMC, Red Hat). The list also includes collapses a momentum system could have held: Lehman, Washington Mutual, Countrywide, Wachovia, Merrill Lynch, Kodak and RadioShack. FMP does not carry these tickers, so the gap cannot be filled from the current data source. Treat the 2000–11 results as optimistic and the 2012–25 results as close to clean.

Fixed in audit

A data-cleaning rule looked at future prices

The panel builder dropped any stock whose highest price was over 1,000× its final price. That rule uses the stock's entire future, and it removed four S&P members that later collapsed: Signature Bank (failed 2023), Sovereign Bancorp (2008), Rite Aid (2000) and Tupperware. The audit panel puts them back; nothing else changes. H never bought them, but they count in the market breadth that drives the pause.

The years-beating figure falls 2–6 points with small changes, so part of H's +4.5 over G comes from picking the best settings out of many. A fair expectation for future years is G plus 1 to 2 points. The drawdown record is the weak part. H's worst fall in the COVID crash was within a point of SPY's, so small changes push many start dates past it.

Note

Profits come from a few trades

The 15 best of 561 trades made 104% of all profit. Western Digital and Seagate in 2025–26 made about $1.4M of the $2.9M ending value. The years-beating measure is not driven by this, but the dollar total is.

What should change

  1. Make the corrected panel the standard: put back the four stocks, and replace the future-looking filter with one that only uses prices up to each day.
  2. Rescore G and H on the corrected panel and pick between them on your yardstick. On corrected data H wins more years (59.0% vs 54.8%), but G keeps the smaller-fall record (78% vs 43%).
  3. Judge the 2000–11 era with caution until a data source with delisted stocks (for example Norgate or CRSP) can fill the survivorship gap.
  4. From now on, test every accepted change on its neighbouring settings as well, and reject it if the result is a lone spike.
