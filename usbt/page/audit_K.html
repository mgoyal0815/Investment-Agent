Audit and trade review · System K · corrected data, Jan 2000 – 12 Oct 2026

System K audit

K's rules use only information available on the day, and its two crash triggers do real work. Its profits, though, come from a handful of long winners. Most short trades lose money, and one inherited rule is tuned to the past.

Verdict

  • No look-ahead: K rerun with the future deleted matched the full run to the cent at all 7 cut-off dates.
  • The crash triggers are real. K beat all 16 runs that held the index on random dates, and random dates added nothing.
  • The cyclicals-vs-defensives restart inherited from H is still tuned to its exact setting. Expect future results 1–3 points of years below the backtest.
  • The survivorship gap from the H audit remains: 2000–11 results are optimistic.
  • Dollar results depend on very few trades: the 10 best of 559 made 98% of all trading profit.

Half of all positions (281 of 559) are sold within 3 months, and 78% of those lose. This is momentum investing as it should work: cut losers fast, let winners run. 2. Pauses, crash holds and restarts

Works

The pauses protect, the crash holds catch rebounds

  • 116 pause episodes, 19 lasting a month or more. Across all pauses SPY made about +4% in total and K +103%: pausing did not cost money overall.
  • 2008–09: paused 195 sessions. SPY fell 37%, K made 5%, helped by three crash holds in the index.
  • 11 crash-trigger index holds during pauses. SPY rose in the next 21 sessions after 9 of them, on average +4.7%: March 2009 +13%, December 2018 +10%, April 2025 +11%. The two misses were October 2008 (−4%) and August 2011 (−2%).
  • 15 cyclicals-vs-defensives restarts during pauses. SPY rose over the next 42 sessions after 80% of them, on average +4.1%.
Cost

Short pauses still flip on and off

97 of the 116 pauses lasted less than a month. Each one sells into weakness and buys back higher. Tests that held the pause state longer (on H) cost more in real turns than they saved.

3. Losing years

K lost to the better of SPY and QQQ in 9 of 26 calendar years: 2006, 2007, 2011, 2013, 2014, 2019, 2020, 2021 and 2023. The average gap was 15.4 points.

Cause of the gap, average losing yearPoints
Missing the giants (average S&P stock vs the index)−9.4

Stock picking (K's stocks vs the average stock)−0.7

Missed giants are now the biggest cost. In those years K held only 27% of the 15 best large stocks at any point. They passed the ADX entry filter on only 35% of decision days, against 70% for the OBV filter and 82% for the trend filter. 2020 and 2023 were the clearest cases: QQQ +49% and +55%, K +15% and +27%. Fixes tested on J (skipping filters, a ranking boost, a size tilt) did not help overall.

In winning years K beat the index by 19.2 points on average, 13.2 of them from stock picking.

What this means

  • K is clean of look-ahead, and each added rule has been tested against luck. Its 67% of years beating SPY and QQQ is a fair backtest figure, but treat about 64–66% as the realistic expectation because of the fitted restart rule and the survivorship gap.
  • Expect long stretches of small losing trades, with occasional big winners making up for them. That is how the system works. Most trades, and most months, will feel like losses.
  • The remaining gap to your 75% bar is mostly missed giants in giant-led markets (2019–21, 2023). Nothing tested so far fixes it without costing more elsewhere.
