US momentum study · 2000 to 12 Oct 2026 · $10,000, whole shares

Can India v8's rules make US v5 beat SPY, QQQ and SMH every year?

No. No combination does it once tested on years it was not tuned on. Three changes do make the US system better, and one of them was chosen strictly on training data.

The years that break every version are semiconductor booms: SMH returned +64% in 2019, +73% in 2023 and +39% in 2024. A diversified stock book does not keep pace with that, however it ranks or sizes.

The study ran the live US v5 code, India v8 ported to US data, each of the 15 differences one at a time, and about 600 combinations. Choices were made on 2012 to 2018 only, then tested on 2019 to October 2026 and on 2000 to 2011.

What changed the US system, and what did not

Candidate 3 (chosen on training data)

The v5 stock engine with India's top-500 universe, India's buy pause instead of the regime overlay, decisions twice a month, and a sell when a holding ranks outside the top 45. Slightly lower returns than v5 in 2019-26, much smaller falls, and 12.9% a year in 2000-11 against 1.6%.

Twice a month beats daily

Deciding on the 11th and the last session instead of every day helped v5 in all three windows. It is the single most consistent change found, but it was identified after the test years were seen.

The tuned winner failed

"Hybrid A" (12 equal slots, India ranking, gold) beat all three indexes in 84% of training years. On 2019-26 it managed 32% with a −51% fall. That gap is overfitting, and it is why the plainer Candidate 3 is the recommendation.

Next steps

  • A survivorship-free dataset (MarketParquet, Norgate or Sharadar) would turn 2000-2018 into a real test. The code reruns unchanged on it.
  • Beating SMH every year needs semiconductor exposure rather than better stock selection. If that is the goal, decide how much of the book should follow the leading index ETF.
